THE IDEA

Useful beats elaborate.

A few small comforts and a personal letter can do more than an expensive assortment. These are ideas for buying items to pack yourself, not merchant-prepared boxes and not a promise that a retailer ships to an APO/FPO address. Ask the recipient what they need and can receive.

01 / ASK

Check what the recipient needs, their unit rules and their current mailing address.

02 / PACK

Choose sturdy, nonperishable, useful items; seal them against leaks and crushing.

03 / CHECK

Review USPS rules for the exact APO/FPO/DPO ZIP, then prepare any customs form.

BEFORE IT GOES

The address changes the answer.

Start with their needs

Military OneSource suggests shelf-stable snacks, toiletries, socks, letters and simple games. Ask the service member about local conditions; what works at one post may not at another. Read its care-package guide ↗

Check the ZIP

USPS restrictions depend on the exact military destination. Its APO/FPO/DPO guide ↗ links the restrictions lookup. Do not assume a seller can ship direct. USPS requires a named recipient, rank/unit, military address and nine-digit ZIP, not “Any Service Member.”

Do the paperwork

Overseas military packages generally need customs forms with item-by-item descriptions. Do not assume perishables, batteries or liquids are accepted; check each item against the destination-specific rules before mailing. USPS customs guidance ↗
